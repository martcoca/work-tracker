## Context

This supersedes [T02](T02-revoke-and-prove.md), which stated that "0000 claims
revocation takes effect within one hour" and required a session to be refused within that
hour. **0000 no longer claims that.**
[ADR-0053](../../../docs/decisions/0053-export-lifetime-matches-the-publishing-budget.md)
moved the export lifetime to 48 hours; a one-hour window obliged 720 publishes a month,
which this organization cannot fund. T02's bound was invalidated the moment that merged.

The rest of T02 stands, and its most important instruction is worth repeating: **the
measured number is the deliverable.** Report it even if it is worse than the stated bound.

This packet also carries the second half of T02's goal, which is unaffected: the product
keeps serving when `0000` is entirely unavailable. That behaviour is now load-bearing in
production — the reader holds its last good copy and fail-closes only at expiry, which is
exactly what took the first deployment down and exactly what must not regress.

## Goal

A revoked grant stops working within the lifetime the export itself declares, measured
against wall-clock time, and the product keeps serving when 0000 is entirely unavailable.

## Boundary

**Read the bound; never hardcode it.** Take it from `contract.FreshnessBound`, which is
half of a two-sided wire contract with 0000's `EXPORT_LIFETIME_MS`. A test that writes
`48` will be silently wrong the next time the window moves — and it has moved once already.

Do not shorten the lifetime, locally or in the fixture, to make the test finish sooner.
If a real timed measurement is impractical in CI, measure it once for real, record the
number, and say in evidence how the CI test differs from the real measurement.

Do not add a revocation push channel. The pull-and-expire model is the design.

## Done when

- A grant is revoked in 0000 and published; a session using it **is refused within
  `contract.FreshnessBound`**, measured against wall-clock time.
- **The measured latency is reported as a real number**, whatever it is.
- Until the held copy expires, the revoked grant **is still honored** — shown deliberately,
  because it is the documented price of ADR-0053 and users will meet it.
- With 0000 entirely unreachable, the product keeps serving from its held copy, and the
  age of that copy is visible to a reader rather than hidden.
- When the held copy finally expires with 0000 still unreachable, the product **fails
  closed** and says why, distinguishably from "unauthenticated" and from "not found".
- The refusal is distinguishable from an expired token and from an unknown tenant.

## Check

```bash
GOWORK=off go test ./... -count=1
```

Plus the timed revocation against live exports, with the measured interval in evidence.

## Evidence

Write `evidence/0004-E03-T03.md`. It must contain the measured revocation latency as a
real wall-clock number, the demonstration that a held copy still honors the revoked grant
until expiry, and the fail-closed behaviour at expiry with 0000 unreachable.
