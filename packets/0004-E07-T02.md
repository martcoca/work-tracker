## Context

`packets.json` is generated from `packets/` at deploy time by `cmd/publish-packet-export`.
Truth therefore flows one way — git to export to reader — and the app is a mirror of the
repository no matter what it stores.

That is why the app cannot become the system of record by adding writes. A packet authored
in the app would not appear in the export, because the export is built from files the app
never touches. Inverting this is the whole of E04's premise.

**T01 must land first.** Publishing from an app that forgets would replace a stale export
with an empty one.

## Goal

The export reflects what the app knows, and a packet authored only in the app reaches
readers without any repository change.

## Boundary

**Do not delete `cmd/publish-packet-export` or stop reading `packets/`.** Both sources must
reconcile during migration: the repository still holds packets that have not moved, and
E04 is what retires them. Removing the git path here would strand every packet not yet
authored in the app.

**Do not weaken the envelope.** Same schema, same digest, same lifetime derived from
`contract.FreshnessBound`. A reader must not be able to tell which side produced an export
except by its provenance field.

**Do not publish on every write.** A packet export republished per comment would spend the
deploy identity's quota and produce a new digest constantly. Publish on a schedule or on
issue, and say which in evidence.

## Done when

- The app publishes `packets.json` from its own store, and the envelope verifies with the
  shipped verifier exactly as the git-produced one does.
- **A packet authored only in the app appears in the export**, shown end to end, with no
  commit to the repository between authoring and appearance.
- Packets still in `packets/` and packets in the store both appear, and a packet present in
  both does not appear twice. State the reconciliation rule in the evidence.
- The provenance field distinguishes an app-published export from a git-published one, so
  which side produced it is a fact rather than an inference.
- **Losing the store is survivable**: with the store unreachable, the service still starts
  from the last good export and refuses to publish, rather than publishing an empty one.
  Show it, including the refusal.
- Publishing holds no long-lived credential. It runs as the runtime identity, which is
  currently zero-permission — say plainly what it must be granted and why that is the
  narrowest grant.
