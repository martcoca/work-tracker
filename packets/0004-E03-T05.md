## Context

[ADR-0057](../../../docs/decisions/0057-the-session-is-not-an-authority-record.md) splits the
two questions that `0004-E03-T04` left joined. A credential proves *who is acting* and *on
what* — it is bound to a packet and an attempt, minted here, on this product's clock. A grant
from `0000` answers *what may this workload do*, and `0000` never sees an attempt.

Today `agentcredential` hardcodes `Kind: "session"` and carries no workload at all, so there
is nothing to look up in the grant export. This packet adds that link, and nothing else.

`0000-E04-T06` publishes the grant this will resolve against. This packet does not wait for
it: a credential naming a workload that holds no grant must be **authenticated and refused**,
which is a rule to prove either way.

## Goal

An authenticated credential yields the workload principal the grant lookup needs, without
weakening the packet and attempt binding it already enforces.

## Boundary

**Do not implement the write operations.** Comment and status transition are `0004-E03-T01`.
This packet ends at "the request carries a workload principal as well as its packet binding".

**Do not put scopes in the credential.** A credential that carries its own permissions cannot
be narrowed after issue. Scope comes from the grant, at request time.

**Do not relax the packet binding.** The credential remains bound to one packet and one
attempt, and that binding stays enforced here — `0000` no longer records it, so this is the
only place it exists.

**Never store, log, or return a credential value.** Unchanged from `0004-E03-T04`: hashed at
rest, shown once, absent from every payload and every export.

## Done when

- Creating a credential **requires naming the workload it acts as** — issuer and subject —
  and refuses a malformed or absent one, distinguishably.
- The workload is returned in credential metadata and is visible when listing, so a human can
  see which authority a credential will resolve against.
- Authentication yields tenant, packet, attempt **and** workload. The workload is what a grant
  lookup uses; the packet and attempt are never sent to `0000`.
- **A credential cannot name a workload outside its tenant**, and the refusal is its own.
- The existing refusals stay distinguishable: unknown, revoked, expired, malformed.
- A valid credential whose workload holds no live grant is **authenticated and refused**,
  distinguishably from "not authenticated".
- Every rule has a test that fails when the rule is removed. Show the failures.
