## Durable path

`packet.EventStore` preserves immutable events at contiguous stream versions and atomically
compares every touched packet's expected version before append. `Tracker` rebuilds its
disposable projection from those records. Single-packet operations refresh only the named
stream; list and explicit rebuild operations replay the complete log.

The Firestore adapter creates one document per event and never updates or deletes it. The
same transaction creates or advances each touched stream's version document. Firestore
retries an aborted transaction; after another writer commits, the callback observes the new
version and returns the model's explicit `ErrConflict`. Supersession includes both packet
streams in one transaction.

Startup still verifies and starts all authority exports before constructing Firestore. A
test supplies an unavailable snapshot and proves that the durable store is never read.
The production binary has no memory-store fallback. Its container smoke fixture implements
only the empty Firestore gRPC query used at startup.

## Infrastructure and cost

The foundation plan adds the `(default)` Firestore Native database in Standard edition at
the configured region. Point-in-time recovery is disabled, database deletion protection is
enabled, and the OpenTofu deletion policy is `ABANDON`. An additive
`google_project_iam_member` grants the runtime only `roles/datastore.user`, conditioned on
that exact database resource.

The tracker cost guard now allowlists those two resource types deliberately. Its source
records the reason: Firestore has no provisioned capacity or warm replica and charges only
for storage and operations, both with a free tier. The guard rejects a non-Standard
database, paid recovery, disabled deletion protection, a broader role, a different member,
or a missing exact-database condition.

The planning envelope is 100 issued packets/month, at most 10 events per packet, 50
single-packet reads/day, and 10 cold starts/day after 1,000 events exist. That is at most
2,000 writes/month (event create plus a conservative stream-counter write per event), 500
ordinary document reads/day, and 10,000 cold-start reads/day. Budgeting 10 MiB/month for
events, counters, and index overhead keeps the first 100 months below 1 GiB.

Firestore Standard's one-free-database allowance includes 1 GiB stored, 50,000 reads/day,
20,000 writes/day, 20,000 deletes/day, and 10 GiB outbound transfer/month, so the expected
envelope is **$0/month**, including data at rest. In `us-central1` after the allowance,
published prices are $0.03/100,000 reads, $0.09/100,000 writes, $0.01/100,000 deletes, and
$0.000205479/GiB-hour (about $0.15/GiB-month) for stored data. Source:
<https://cloud.google.com/firestore/pricing>.

## A clause this packet should not have carried

The original Done-when also required a packet authored through the signed-in human surface
to survive a forced cold start. That clause is **withdrawn**, and the reasoning belongs in
the record rather than in a quiet edit.

It had no actor who could satisfy it. The Founder does not author packets — human operation
here is review — so it tested the write path nobody uses. The chief-of-staff cannot
authenticate, and minting a token is refused by its own permission boundary. A worker that
tried to satisfy it reached for the Founder's already-signed-in browser session, which is
now forbidden outright (`doctrine/worker/entrypoint.md`).

It was also redundant. Durability across a new client is proven above, at the store, against
the real database. A browser round trip would have re-proven the weakest third of it.

The end-to-end write is still worth proving, and it is now `0004-E03-T01`'s: a *session*
writes through the session API and the write survives. That tests the path this product
exists for, and needs no human and no borrowed authority.
